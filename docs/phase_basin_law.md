# Phase Basin Law

The **Phase Basin Law** states that system evolution is governed by
discrete constraint-defined basins of stability. Transitions between
basins are not driven by local dynamics alone, but are permitted only
by the global topology of the constraint field.

## Core statement

> State transitions are topology-gated, not force-driven.

## Interpretation

In this view:

- Dynamics describe motion **within** a basin
- Topology determines **when transitions are possible**
- Timing is therefore **topology-locked, not dynamics-driven**

## Implications

This implies that:

- Recovery cannot occur unless a valid basin exists
- Collapse corresponds to exiting all viable basins
- Stability is a property of constraint geometry, not merely energy input

## Relation to GV

Within the GV framework, basin membership is reflected in the behavior
of the God Variable (GV):

- **Stable GV** indicates basin residence
- **Oscillating GV** suggests boundary proximity
- **Collapse in GV** signals basin transition or loss of viable topology

## Summary

The Phase Basin Law reframes system change as a constraint-topology problem.
A system does not transition simply because forces push it. It transitions
only when the structure of the constraint field allows a new viable basin
to exist.
